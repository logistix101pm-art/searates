What actually drives the price of an ocean shipment

Ocean freight pricing turns on four variables, and a freight forwarder quote that omits any of them is not comparable with another quote. Google's own AI answers for this category name the same variables: origin and destination ports appear in 16 of the 48 answers SeaRates captured on 07/26/2026, and the FCL-or-LCL choice in 10.

  • Load type. An ocean freight rate prices a full container (FCL) per container. Less-than-container-load (LCL) is priced per cubic metre or per revenue ton, whichever bills higher — which is why an LCL quote can beat an FCL quote on paper and lose on the invoice.
  • Port pair. Ocean freight rates are lane-specific. A rate from Shanghai to Los Angeles tells you nothing useful about Shanghai to New York, and the gap between the two moves independently of either.
  • Service scope. Port-to-port, door-to-port and door-to-door are three different products. Most published rates are port-to-port.
  • Timing. Spot rates reprice weekly. A quote's validity window is part of its price.

Two ocean shipments of identical volume on the same lane can differ by a multiple once these four are settled differently. Normalising them to the same scope is the first step of any honest comparison, and it is the step a rate table cannot do for you. A freight forwarder returning a number without naming all four has not answered the question that was asked.

Benchmark rates by lane, with the date they were taken

An ocean freight benchmark is only meaningful with its date attached, because container rates in 2026 have moved faster than quarterly reporting can track. Public indices measure different things: Drewry's World Container Index and Freightos' FBX both publish spot composites, Xeneta publishes contracted as well as spot data, and none of them is your quote.

Read a published index as context, not as a price:

  • Freight rate indices are composites across many shippers and volumes. A small shipper's quote sits above the index more often than below it.
  • The common indices publish 40ft container rates. No comparable public index exists for 20ft containers, so a 20ft "index rate" you are shown was derived by someone, and the derivation is the thing to ask about.
  • Surcharges usually sit outside the index number. Terminal handling, bunker adjustment and peak-season surcharges land on the invoice.

Ocean freight benchmarks disagree by more than most quotes do, and the disagreement is measurable rather than rhetorical. On one trade, within nine days, three publishers gave three numbers:

Freight forwarder rankings in the United States come from four kinds of publisher, and they measure four different things. Armstrong & Associates ranks by revenue and volume. Review platforms such as Trustpilot and Clutch aggregate customer ratings for freight forwarders and for everyone else alike. Directory sites list companies that pay for placement. None of the four publisher types measures whether a freight forwarder holds the license that governs your ocean leg. That gap is measurable: across 245 pages ranking in US search that SeaRates read on 07/26/2026, 26.5 % mention the Federal Maritime Commission, an OTI license or an NVOCC at all, and 0.8 % show a license number.

Freight forwarder comparison is not one job but three, and most publishers do only the first. Naming freight forwarders is easy, scoring them requires published criteria, and verifying them requires a federal lookup nobody is paid to perform. Of the 62 freight forwarder ranking pages SeaRates read on 07/26/2026, three published criteria and none stated how many companies were considered before the list was cut, which leaves the difference between a shortlist drawn from ten candidates and one drawn from ten thousand invisible to the reader.

A freight forwarder ranking by revenue also answers a question most shippers are not asking. A freight forwarder in the global top 25 may be an excellent fit for a 500-container programme and a poor fit for two pallets to Rotterdam, and the ranking cannot distinguish the two cases. Our segment pages exist because that distinction decides the outcome: how ocean freight forwarders compare on our scorecard covers the general case, and how the China to USA forwarder lists are built covers the busiest US import lane, where 0 of the 31 pages we measured publish a ranking method.

How we score a company: FMC license first, USDOT never

A freight forwarder's score on SeaRates starts with the credential that governs the ocean leg, not with the credential that is easiest to display. The Federal Maritime Commission licenses ocean transportation intermediaries; a USDOT or MC number comes from FMCSA and covers motor carriage. Both are real, and only one of them is about your container.

Scoring runs in this order:

  • License. FMC OTI record, its number, its status, and whether the entry sits in the freight forwarder list, the NVOCC list, or both.
  • Financial responsibility. The bond on file — $50,000 for an ocean freight forwarder, $75,000 for a licensed US-based NVOCC, $150,000 for a registered foreign-based NVOCC — read as a screening signal, not as insurance.
  • Price evidence. Quotes and invoices we hold for that company's lanes, reduced to medians with p25 and p75.
  • Disclosure. Whether the company publishes its license number where a customer can see it. Two of the 245 pages we measured do.

Each shipping company check is dated on the page it appears on. Companies are re-checked on a fixed cycle, and a status change moves the entry before it moves the ranking. The full procedure, including how to run it yourself in about two minutes, sits on verify a shipping company license.

Three segments, and why we keep them apart

Ocean freight, car shipping and household moves are three different purchases with three different failure modes, and merging them into one ranking helps nobody. A business importing pallets needs customs capability and lane coverage. A person shipping a car needs RoRo or container expertise and export compliance. A family relocating needs origin packing, destination customs and a survey.

  • Freight forwarders — commercial cargo, FCL and LCL, customs brokerage, lane coverage.
  • Car shipping — RoRo and container methods, export documentation, destination import rules: what sets the bill for shipping a vehicle overseas.
  • International movers — household goods, survey and packing, two regulators across the two legs: the overseas moving section.

Ocean freight, vehicles and household goods were measured separately for the same reason they are ranked separately, and each corpus fails its readers in its own way. In the car shipping corpus, 41 of 47 pages name no regulator at all. In the moving corpus, 12 of 67 name a destination-side cost, 17.9% of the pages measured. Among the 27 mover ranking pages, 11 name a USDOT number against 5 naming the FMC. Three markets, three different blind spots, and a single merged ranking would see none of them.

How we make money: no paid placement, and no referral link today

Ocean freight booked through any company listed here pays SeaRates nothing: not one of the 150 outbound links on this site is a referral link, and no company has paid to appear on it. If that changes, the rate will be published on the page that carries the link and will be identical for every company in the list. Rank cannot be bought, positions are not sold, and no company sees its score before publication.

Conflict of interest in freight forwarder rankings is real enough that Google's own AI answer for the head query of this category raises it directly, listing conflict of interest among the things to check about a ranking site. The answer to it is structural rather than verbal: with no referral link on the site there is nothing to order the list by, and the standing policy — one rate, published, identical across all listings — removes the incentive even when a link appears, because the payment would not vary with the order.

Ocean freight disclosure appears where the decision is made. Every comparison table carries a column showing whether a listing is monetised, and every form states what happens to the data before you fill it in — not in a footer, and not on a separate policy page.

How to tell a verifiable review from a manufactured one

Shipping company reviews are worth reading and worth discounting, and the difference comes down to what a reviewer can prove. Reddit holds a top-10 position for 47 % of the queries in this category, which is a useful signal in itself: shippers trust a forum thread with specifics over a five-star average.

  • Check the detail. An ocean freight review naming the lane, the container size, the month and the surcharge that surprised them is describing a real shipment. A review praising "great service" is describing nothing.
  • Check the distribution. Genuine review sets have a spread. A wall of five-star ratings posted within a short window is a campaign.
  • Check the entity. Compare review dates against the company's registration date. Reviews predating the legal entity belong to a predecessor, and the predecessor's record is the one worth finding.

Shipping company review platforms are also businesses. Profile features, response tools and badges are sold to the companies being reviewed, which does not make the reviews false but does explain why almost every profile converges on the same rating band.

Ocean freight review sets are also smaller than their averages suggest. Of the 26 machine-readable ratings SeaRates found in the company-check cluster on 07/26/2026, twelve rest on fewer than 100 reviews, and across the five companies rated on two or more platforms the median gap between a company's highest and lowest score was 1.9 points out of 5. A rating carried from one platform to another is not the same claim it was on the platform that produced it.

Shipping company reviews in this category sit on a corpus that can be counted: SeaRates captured the 177 pages ranking across the 49 queries on 07/26/2026, spread over 127 domains, 102 of which hold a single URL. Of those, 17 pages sit on platforms that sell the companies they list a profile, a badge or accreditation — Clutch, GoodFirms, the Better Business Bureau, Trustpilot, G2 and Yelp — and 12 pages of them are ranked lists of the kind a shipper reads as a recommendation. Just 3 pages are a register lookup where the licence behind a name can be checked: the FMC's two OTI searches and the FMCSA's SAFER snapshot. Classified by domain by SeaRates on 07/28/2026. For every page in this category that lets a reader verify a company, four rank it on a platform that sells that company its own profile.

What a price comparison does not show you

The gap between a quoted ocean freight rate and the money that leaves your account is the least visible part of this market, and it is where comparisons break. Three categories account for most of it:

  • Origin and destination charges on an ocean shipment. Terminal handling, documentation and drayage are frequently quoted separately or not at all.
  • Adjustment surcharges. Bunker adjustment factors and peak-season surcharges are applied to the base rate after booking.
  • Detention and demurrage. Demurrage runs while a container sits inside the terminal past its free time. Detention runs while you hold the carrier's box outside it. Confusing the two is the single most common question in this category's People Also Ask results.

Ocean freight quotes hide that gap in plain sight, and the share of the bill it accounts for is almost never stated. Across the 299 landed cost pages SeaRates measured on 07/26/2026, exactly five put a number on the ocean rate's share of the delivered total, and the range those five give runs from a third to two thirds. A comparison of ocean rates alone therefore compares somewhere between 33% and 67% of the money, with no way to tell which end you are on from the quote itself.

One rule changed the ocean freight balance here in favour of shippers. Under the Federal Maritime Commission's demurrage and detention billing rule, effective 05/28/2024, an invoice that omits a required element is one the billed party is no longer obliged to pay. Most shippers do not know the rule exists, and most invoices are never checked against it. The required elements, and how to challenge an invoice that lacks them, are set out under demurrage and detention charges — the invoice has 30 days to arrive and you have at least 30 to dispute it; what the delivered total contains line by line sits under duty and customs fees on top of the freight.

The question that brings most readers here is narrower than any of this, and it has a bad answer in the market: who is the cheapest freight forwarder, and why no published list can say works through the 62 ranking pages that claim to answer it and the four questions that actually settle a comparison.

Who publishes ocean freight data in the US?

Drewry publishes the World Container Index, Freightos publishes the FBX, and Xeneta publishes contracted and spot benchmarks. All three are composites across many shippers, and none of them is a quote for your shipment.

How do freight comparison sites make money?

Most earn referral fees from listed companies, some sell placement, and some sell profile features to the companies being compared. The question to ask is whether the fee varies with position, because that is what turns a comparison into an advertisement.

Are freight forwarder rankings trustworthy?

A ranking is trustworthy to the extent its basis is published. Rankings by revenue measure size, review aggregates measure sentiment, and directories measure who paid. None of them measures whether a freight forwarder holds the license covering your ocean leg.

How do I compare freight forwarders properly?

Normalise the freight forwarder quotes to the same scope first — load type, port pair, service scope and validity. Then check the license and bond, then compare price against a dated benchmark rather than against the other quote alone.

Is there a Consumer Reports for shipping companies?

No consumer body rates ocean freight forwarders. Federal records cover licensing and financial responsibility, review platforms cover sentiment, and neither covers service quality on a specific lane.

Who is the cheapest freight forwarder?

No freight forwarder is cheapest across lanes, and a company that is cheapest on one port pair is routinely mid-market on another. Compare on your lane, on a dated median, and check what the quote excludes.

How do I know if a freight forwarder is legit?

Check the FMC OTI registry for the license and the bond, match the registry name against the invoice entity, and confirm which party issues the bill of lading. All three checks are free and take minutes.

What are the performance metrics of a freight forwarder?

On-time arrival against the original schedule, quote-to-invoice variance, claims resolution time and documentation accuracy. Revenue and TEU volume, the metrics most rankings use, describe the company's size rather than your outcome.
