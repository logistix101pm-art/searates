What does it cost to ship a car to Nigeria from USA?

Ocean freight to Nigeria runs $1,200 to $2,800 per vehicle before destination charges, and the same move delivered runs $2,700 to $4,500, both read by SeaRates on 07/26/2026. California to Nigeria, quoted as a single origin, is published at $2,195 to $3,590.

Vehicle shipping quotes name the ocean leg, not the cost of the move. The gap between the two rows is $1,500 to $1,700 per car, so the Nigerian side costs roughly as much again as the voyage. Vehicle shipping quotes are built from the first row and paid from the second.

Published ranges are not medians of invoices. Nobody in this market publishes a median, and neither do we yet. Readers comparing destinations can start from what sets the bill for shipping a vehicle overseas.

RoRo or container: what the choice changes in price and in risk

RoRo costs $1,000 to $2,000 while a shared container runs $1,500 to $3,000, and the choice changes more than the invoice. RoRo means the car is driven onto the vessel under its own power; a container means it is strapped inside a steel box, alone or with other vehicles.

Which details of your car set the price?

Make, model and year set the base rate before engine size adjusts it. A carrier prices the space a vehicle occupies and the risk it carries, so quotation questions are consistent across operators: year, make and model first, then engine displacement, then which side the steering wheel is on, then whether the car starts and drives.

Non-running vehicles cannot go RoRo at all and move to containers at a higher rate. Engine displacement is the driver operators ask about and pages omit: 6 of 137 pages measured on 07/26/2026 mention it. Left-hand drive matters at the destination rather than at the ports of loading, and for Nigeria it is decisive.

What the US government requires before the ship loads

Federal rules require the export filing 72 hours before loading for every used vehicle, with no exception for cheap cars or friendly destinations. US Customs and Border Protection states that Electronic Export Information must reach the Automated Export System for all used self-propelled vehicles regardless of destination, value or condition, citing 78 FR 16366 and 15 CFR Part 30.

Proof of ownership runs on a parallel track under 19 CFR Part 192. Vehicle export therefore has two documentary gates:

  1. The EEI filing. Submitted through AES or AESDirect at least 72 hours before the vessel loads.
  2. The ITN. Issued by AES against a specific VIN once the filing is accepted, and the only real evidence the declaration exists.
  3. Proof of ownership. The title, or a lien release when a lender holds it, presented under 19 CFR Part 192.

The 72-hour rule is not paperwork a forwarder can start after the booking is confirmed. Only 8 of 137 pages measured by SeaRates on 07/26/2026 mention 19 CFR 192 or the title requirement at all, which is why sellers meet it as a surprise rather than a schedule.

Duty, tax and the age limit at the other end

Nigeria's official vehicle age cap is 12 years, cut from 15 in 2022 under the Nigeria Customs Service's Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) Valuation Policy — making 2014 the oldest eligible model year in 2026. That is the codified national rule, consistent across Nigerian customs and industry press through 2026. A 10-year figure also circulates, but it is not a competing rule: the same sources describe individual ports applying a stricter, non-uniform 10-year cutoff (2016) in practice, so a car that clears one port can be turned away at another. Sources do agree on the rule that stops more shipments than age: Nigeria permits left-hand drive vehicles only.

Vehicles entering Ghana meet the one corridor here with a primary document behind its duties: 27 of 137 pages state a destination age rule at all, and Ghana publishes its own. The Ghana Revenue Authority applies depreciation of 0 to 50% by vehicle age to the FOB value, computes duty from that base, and adds VAT at a standard 15% plus NHIL/GETFund, an EXIM levy, a processing fee and a special import levy. Duty varies by tariff code — buses carrying ten or more people sit at 5%, motorcycles at 20% — and a vehicle can be cleared without an agent by registering with the GRA Customs Division.

Cars entering the United States follow a different rulebook: EPA form 3520-1 and DOT form HS-7 declare the emissions and safety basis of the import, and vehicles older than 25 years are exempt from federal safety and emissions standards altogether.

Which US port is cheaper, and what the drive to it adds

Published rates differ by hundreds of dollars between West Coast, Gulf and East Coast ports, for the same car to the same destination. Operators serving West Africa publish per-port rate lines rather than one national price.

How long does it take, and why not sooner than 72 hours?

The export filing sets the floor: no vehicle can lawfully load within 72 hours of the EEI submission, so the regulator, not the carrier's schedule, decides the earliest sailing a car can make. Booking a vessel that leaves tomorrow does not shorten that window.

After loading, transit and destination clearance add the rest. SeaRates has not measured median or p90 transit on the USA to Lagos corridor. Vendor pages actually read for this cluster spread wider than a single range: RoRoFromUSA publishes 25-40 days, Schumacher Cargo 30-40 days, and WC Shipping 40 days from the East Coast against 50 from California — all vendor claims, not a SeaRates finding. Vehicle export timing is best planned backwards from the sailing date, with three days of regulatory lead time treated as fixed — a constraint 11 of 137 pages mention.

What the quote does not cover

Car shipping quotes exclude destination clearing, port charges and agent fees, which roughly double the ocean freight figure. Almost none of it appears in the quote that wins the booking. Car shipping quotes are built to be comparable at the point of sale, so the parts that vary by country sit outside them.

Is the company allowed to move your car across an ocean?

Car shipping pages rarely name a regulator: of 137 pages ranking here, five mention the licence that governs the ocean leg. SeaRates read the full text of every competing page across the 60 queries in this cluster on 07/26/2026: 5 pages (3.6%) name the Federal Maritime Commission or an OTI licence, 2 (1.5%) name a USDOT number, and 94.9% name no regulator at all.

A USDOT number governs a truck moving between US states and says nothing about the ocean leg. An FMC Ocean Transportation Intermediary licence covers arranging the sea carriage — the part that actually crosses water. Car shipping is sold as one service and performed by several companies, so the credential has to match the leg.

Checking a car shipping company takes a minute. The FMC registry held 9,795 organisations as of the 28/07/2026 official export, of which 5,205 carried an active licence number (675 forwarder-only, 1,942 NVOCC-only, 2,588 both roles); the registry's own web search page shows a different, larger count because it tallies trade-name listings rather than organisations. Ask which company performs the ocean leg, then look up that name rather than the one on the website. The same discipline drives the licence and bond behind the company name, and operators that pass it appear in our car shipping ranking.

What goes wrong, and who pays for it

Cars can leave the USA lawfully and still be refused entry, and the bill for storage and re-export goes to the sender — a consequence 0 of 137 pages state. Vehicle export failures cluster into a short list, each with a check that costs nothing beforehand:

  • Wrong side steering. A right-hand drive car bought cheaply for Nigeria cannot be landed. Confirm the destination rule before purchase.
  • Age over the cap. Model year is checked at the destination against a rule that only 27 of 137 competing pages state at all.
  • Title held by a lender. Without a lien release the terminal will not load the unit, and storage begins immediately.
  • No accepted filing. Ask for the ITN and the AES acceptance against your VIN.

Verifying an export is a document check, not a matter of trust: the ITN is issued per VIN, so a seller who cannot produce one has not filed.

What is the ITN number for car export?

The Internal Transaction Number issued by the Automated Export System when an EEI filing is accepted. Each ITN is tied to one VIN, which makes it the practical proof that a declaration was filed for your car.

How to check if a vehicle is exported?

Request the ITN and the AES acceptance for your VIN from whoever filed the declaration. A booking confirmation, an invoice or a draft bill of lading do not show that an export declaration exists.
