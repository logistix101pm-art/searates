How much does a 50 lb package cost to ship, and why this site cannot tell you

By SeaRates · Updated 29 July 2026 · Every figure here carries its unit, the date it was read and the corpus behind it. Corrections with a source: corrections@searates.top

⚑ Three markets, one phrase

A 50-pound package is a courier shipment, and courier pricing works on a different principle from everything on this site: chargeable weight against a zone table, published by the carrier and quotable in seconds on its own website. Ocean freight, which is what SeaRates measures, becomes relevant somewhere above two cubic metres. Publishing a parcel figure here would mean quoting a market we do not measure — so instead, here is how to tell which of the three markets your shipment belongs to.

A 50-pound package is in the first row. The rate is published by the courier, varies by destination zone and service level, and is quotable directly without an intermediary.

Chargeable weight, which is the part that surprises people

Couriers bill on the greater of actual weight and dimensional weight. Dimensional weight is the box volume divided by a published divisor, so a light bulky parcel bills as if it were heavier.

This is the same principle as the ocean weight-or-measure rule, applied at parcel scale. Two 50-pound shipments in differently sized boxes cost different amounts, and the box is the variable you control.

When a parcel question is really an ocean question

Several shipments a month to the same destination, or a single consignment above roughly two cubic metres, changes the arithmetic. Ocean carries fixed costs that a parcel does not — a customs entry at $100-250, an ISF filing at $35-100, a destination CFS charge — and those only make sense spread over volume.

If you are consolidating parcels because the per-parcel cost has become the largest line in your landed cost, that is the point at which the ocean pages here start to answer your question.

What this site does publish

Rate benchmarks with the date each was read, the line-by-line composition of a delivered container, free-time and demurrage tariffs, and how to check that whoever is arranging the ocean leg holds the licence for it. What it does not publish is a courier zone table, because we do not measure that market and a figure without a measurement behind it is the thing this site exists to argue against.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 50 lb package cost to ship?

That is a courier rate, priced on chargeable weight against a destination zone table and quotable directly on the carrier’s site. SeaRates measures ocean freight and does not publish parcel rates.

What is chargeable weight?

The greater of actual weight and dimensional weight, where dimensional weight is the box volume divided by a published divisor. A light bulky parcel therefore bills as if it were heavier.

When is ocean freight cheaper than a courier?

Generally above about two cubic metres, once the fixed ocean costs — entry at $100-250, ISF at $35-100, CFS handling — can be spread over enough volume.

Can I ship one box by sea?

You can, and it is rarely economic. The customs entry, the ISF and the CFS handling charge do not shrink with the cargo, so a single carton carries the same fixed costs as a pallet.

Is a freight forwarder useful for parcels?

Not usually. Parcel rates are published and bookable directly; a forwarder adds value where filings, consolidation and liability across an ocean leg are involved.
