What are the top freight companies in the US, when most of the biggest are not American?

By SeaRates · Updated 13 September 2026 · Every figure here carries its unit, the date it was read and the corpus behind it. Corrections with a source: corrections@searates.top

⚑ US-based against US-serving

The question hides an ambiguity worth separating. Of the nine forwarders present in both Armstrong & Associates 2025 tables, two are headquartered in the United States: C.H. Robinson at 1,256,000 ocean TEUs and $14,768M revenue, and Expeditors at 855,200 TEUs and $11,069M. Every one of the nine serves US cargo. If the question is who moves the most American freight, headquarters is the wrong variable — and licence status is the right one.

Why headquarters is the wrong filter

A forwarder arranging ocean carriage for US cargo needs US authority regardless of where it is incorporated. An ocean freight forwarder must be US-based to hold that licence; an NVOCC may be foreign-based, in which case it is registered rather than licensed and posts a $150,000 bond against $75,000.

So the useful question is not whose flag is on the letterhead but which entity holds the credential for your leg. The register held 9,795 organisations as of the 28/07/2026 official export, of which 5,205 carried an active licence number, and it answers that in about two minutes.

Frequently asked questions

What are the top 5 freight companies in the US?

By ocean volume among the nine in the Armstrong 2025 tables, the largest serving the US are Sinotrans, Kuehne+Nagel, DSV, DHL Global Forwarding and Cosco Shipping Logistics. Only C.H. Robinson and Expeditors are US-headquartered.

Which US freight forwarder is the biggest?

C.H. Robinson, at 1,256,000 ocean TEUs and $14,768M revenue in 2025, ahead of Expeditors at 855,200 TEUs and $11,069M.

Does a forwarder have to be American to ship US cargo?

An ocean freight forwarder must be US-based to hold the FMC licence. An NVOCC may be foreign-based, registered rather than licensed, and posts a $150,000 bond.

How many licensed forwarders are there in the US?

The FMC's own official export (28/07/2026) counts 9,795 organisations in the OTI registry, of which 5,205 carry an active licence — 675 forwarder-only, 1,942 NVOCC-only, 2,588 both. These are deduplicated by organisation number, unlike the registry's own web-search page.

Is a bigger forwarder better for a small importer?

Not automatically. Scale buys allocation when capacity tightens; it does not buy attention, and the destination-side charges that dominate a small shipment are the same either way.
