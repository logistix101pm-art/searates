Who is the biggest freight forwarder in the world? It depends which column you read

By SeaRates · Updated 13 September 2026 · Every figure here carries its unit, the date it was read and the corpus behind it. Corrections with a source: corrections@searates.top

⚑ Two orderings, one question

There is no single largest freight forwarder, and the two honest answers point at different companies. Ranked by ocean containers moved in 2025, Sinotrans is first and sits seventh on revenue. Ranked by revenue, DSV is first and sits third on volume. Both orderings come from the same publisher, Armstrong & Associates, and neither measures whether the company answers the phone.

Why the two tables disagree

Ocean volume counts twenty-foot equivalent units handled. Revenue counts money passing through the company, which includes air freight, contract logistics and customs brokerage — businesses with entirely different unit economics. A forwarder heavy in air freight books large revenue on small volume; a forwarder concentrated in ocean does the opposite.

Neither is a trick. They are answers to different questions, and a list quoting one while implying the other is where the confusion starts. Across the 62 ranking pages SeaRates measured, none publishes both columns side by side.

What either ranking does not tell a US importer

Both tables measure the size of the seller, not the quality of the transaction. Nothing in either column says whether the company holds a current US licence for the ocean leg, what its bond covers, or how it behaves when a container misses its free time.

The licence part is checkable in about two minutes. The Federal Maritime Commission's registry held 9,795 organisations as of the 28/07/2026 official export, of which 5,205 carried an active licence number, and the search is free. Of 245 pages SeaRates measured across the ranking clusters, 2 show an actual OTI number and 4 point the reader at the database at all.

Size is a weak proxy for the thing you are buying

For a shipper moving a handful of containers a year, a top-ten forwarder's scale mostly buys allocation during a squeeze — real, but seasonal — and mostly does not buy attention. The lines that decide the bill are the same either way: terminal handling at $138-340 per container per end, brokerage at $100-250 per entry, and whatever the quote left for the destination.

What size does change is recourse. A larger intermediary is likelier to still exist when a claim lands, and likelier to carry the bond category that matters: $50,000 for a licensed ocean freight forwarder, $75,000 for a US-based NVOCC, $150,000 for a registered foreign-based one. The bond is a shared pool across all claimants, not insurance on your shipment.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the biggest freight forwarder in the world?

By ocean containers moved in 2025, Sinotrans. By revenue in the same year, DSV. Both figures come from Armstrong & Associates, and the two orderings differ by six places for Sinotrans.

Who is the number one freight forwarder in the world?

There is no single number one. The question resolves only once you name the metric — ocean volume, total revenue, air tonnage or contract logistics — and the leader changes with it.

Is DHL or Kuehne+Nagel bigger?

It depends on the column. Air and contract logistics revenue rank differently from ocean TEUs, and any answer that does not name which one it used is answering a different question.

Does the biggest forwarder give the best rates?

Scale buys allocation during a capacity squeeze rather than a standing discount. For shippers below roughly 50 containers a year, the destination-side lines move the total far more than the forwarder margin does.

How do I check a forwarder is licensed in the US?

Search the company and its trade name in the FMC Ocean Transportation Intermediary application. A US ocean freight forwarder must be US-based; a foreign NVOCC appears as registered rather than licensed and carries no licence number.
