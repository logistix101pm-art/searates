How to find a reliable freight forwarder, when reliability is the one thing nobody publishes

⚑ Facts you can get, adjectives you cannot

Reliability is not published anywhere, by anyone, including us: no regulator collects on-time performance by forwarder and no publisher counts complaints against shipments. What is published is authority, bond and liability — and those three settle most of what people mean when they ask this. Yes, they are legal — and the licence is the first check

A freight forwarder arranging ocean carriage for US cargo is a regulated business, not an unregulated middleman. The authority is an ocean transportation intermediary licence from the Federal Maritime Commission, and it comes with a bond and a named qualifying individual with industry experience behind it.

That makes the first check binary rather than a matter of judgement. Search the entity in the FMC Ocean Transportation Intermediary application, by company name and by trade name, because one licensee can operate under several trade names. A US-based entity returns a licence number. A foreign-based NVOCC appears as registered, carries no licence number and posts a larger bond, $150,000 against $75,000 — that is expected, not a red flag. Absence from both lists, for a company arranging ocean carriage for US cargo, is the finding.

The last two are the ones that separate a workable quote from a number. A forwarder that answers all five in writing has told you more than any review page can.

What people mean by "reliable", and which part is checkable

The word usually carries three different worries at once, and they separate cleanly:

  • Will the company still exist when a claim lands? Partly checkable. The bond category and the

registry entry are evidence of a threshold cleared; company age and the state business registry add to it. Nothing proves solvency.

  • Will the cargo arrive when they said? Not checkable by forwarder. Schedule reliability is

measured by carrier, not by intermediary: Sea-Intelligence put global on-time performance at 64.7% for May 2026, and that number belongs to the vessel operator, not to whoever booked the slot.

  • Will the invoice match the quote? Checkable in advance, by asking for the exclusions. This is

where most of the disappointment in the segment actually lives.

Frequently asked questions

How to find a reliable freight forwarder?

Run five checks: the FMC OTI register for authority, the bond category, which entity issues the bill of lading, the written exclusions from the quote, and the validity window. All are free and return facts rather than adjectives.

How to pick a freight forwarder?

Shortlist on authority and liability, then decide on the completeness of the quote. Two quotes normalised to the same scope are comparable; two quotes with different scopes are not, whatever the headline figures say.

Are freight forwarders legal?

Yes. Arranging ocean carriage for US cargo requires an FMC ocean transportation intermediary licence, a bond and a qualifying individual. Foreign-based NVOCCs are registered rather than licensed and post a $150,000 bond.

How to find a freight forwarder from China?

The entity that matters is the one with US authority, because that is the side where your liability and your entry sit. Ask which US-licensed entity issues the bill of lading and search that name, not the Chinese trading name.

Can I check a forwarder without paying anything?

Every check above is free and needs no account. The federal applications are public, and the two commercial questions — exclusions and validity — are answered by the forwarder in writing.
