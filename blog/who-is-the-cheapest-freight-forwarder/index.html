Who is the cheapest freight forwarder, and why no published list can answer it

By SeaRates · Updated 13 September 2026 · Every figure here carries its unit, the date it was read and the corpus behind it. Corrections with a source: corrections@searates.top

⚑ The question under the question

No published list can name a cheapest freight forwarder, and the ones that try are ranking something else. A forwarder does not set the ocean rate: it buys space from a carrier and adds its own lines on top. Two quotes for the same container differ mostly in what they leave out, which is why the cheaper number on paper routinely bills higher on arrival. What "cheapest" is measuring when a list says it

A freight forwarder resells ocean carriage. It holds no vessel, and on most lanes it holds no rate advantage a competitor cannot also buy. What differs between two forwarders is the margin, the handling lines and — decisively — how much of the shipment the quote actually covers.

That makes "cheapest freight forwarder" a question about documents rather than about companies. The forwarder quoting $2,600 port-to-port and the forwarder quoting $3,900 door-to-door may be charging the same margin on the same slot.

What "cheapest" is measuring when a list says it
What the quote saysWhat is usually insideWhat arrives later
Port to portOcean freight, carrier surchargesTerminal handling, brokerage, ISF, drayage, duty
Door to portPickup, export clearance, ocean freightDestination terminal, brokerage, duty, delivery

Normalising two quotes to the same scope is the whole exercise, and it is the step a ranking cannot do on your behalf.

What the market publishes instead

Of the 62 pages SeaRates measured for these queries, 3 explain how their order was arrived at. The rest present a shortlist and describe each entry in adjectives. Nine of the ten most common criteria named across those pages — "reliable", "global network", "great customer service" — are unfalsifiable by construction: nothing about them can be checked before or after a booking.

Where a page does name a number, it is usually a lane rate quoted without a week attached. Ocean spot rates reprice weekly; Drewry's World Container Index stood at $4,374 per 40-foot container in the assessment of 07/23/2026, down 4% on the week. A "cheapest" claim carrying no date is not wrong so much as unreadable.

The lines that actually decide the total

Across the 299 pages measured for landed cost, the same lines recur, and their published ranges are wide enough to swamp any margin difference between two forwarders.

The lines that actually decide the total

Terminal handling (THC)$138-340 per container, each endCarrier or terminal
Customs brokerage entry$100-250 per entryCustoms broker
ISF filing$35-100 per shipmentBroker or forwarder

Ocean freight itself is between 35% and 70% of the delivered total, depending on whether the shipment is consolidated or full-container. The corollary is blunt: a forwarder shaving 5% off the ocean line while leaving $600 of destination charges unnamed is not the cheaper one.

What to ask instead, and in what order

The comparable question is not who is cheapest but whose quote is complete. Four questions settle it, and any forwarder can answer all four in writing:

  • Which entity issues the bill of lading? That names who is liable, and whether you are dealing

with an agent or a carrier.

  • Which of the lines above are excluded? Ask for the exclusions, not the inclusions.
  • What is the validity window? A rate without an expiry is not a rate.
  • What happens on day six? Free time is 3 to 7 days at most US terminals, and the clock is the

fastest-moving cost in the whole file.

Run all four against two quotes and the cheaper one is usually obvious — and is usually not the one with the smaller headline.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the cheapest freight forwarder?

No list can name one, because a forwarder resells carrier space and the difference between two quotes is mostly scope, not price. Of 62 ranking pages measured on 07/25-26/2026, 3 publish a method and none compares quotes for one identical shipment.

Who has the cheapest freight shipping?

For a given lane and week, the carrier rate is broadly the same wherever you buy it. What varies is what the quote leaves for the destination: terminal handling at $138-340, brokerage at $100-250 and ISF at $35-100 land on top of it whoever booked.

Which freight is the cheapest?

By unit cost, a full container beats less-than-container load once the volume passes roughly 13-15 CBM; below that, LCL is usually cheaper. LCL is priced per cubic metre or per revenue ton, whichever bills higher, which is why the crossover moves with density.

Is it cheaper to book a forwarder or go direct to the carrier?

Carriers quote contract rates on volume most importers do not have, and they do not file your customs entry. Going direct removes the forwarder margin and adds the work the forwarder was doing. Below roughly 50 containers a year it rarely nets out cheaper.

What is a freight forwarder FMCSA?

FMCSA registers freight forwarders for the domestic road leg. It is a different agency from the Federal Maritime Commission, which licenses ocean transportation intermediaries. A USDOT number says nothing about who is liable at sea.
