For these shipping companies the register column is the point rather than a gap. Four of the six brands are couriers, and no absence from the ocean register says anything about them — which is exactly why a single ranking of these six would be meaningless.

One courier brand shows how far the confusion reaches. DHL Express is a courier and holds no OTI licence; DHL Global Forwarding is a separate business in the ocean market, and the federal register carries it as a registered trade name of DANZAS CORPORATION under OTI licence 000315. The same three letters therefore sit in two of the three markets on this page, under two different legal persons, with two different regulators.

Couriers, carriers and movers can only be compared inside one market, so the first decision is which market a shipment belongs to. The rule of thumb is the shipment itself rather than the destination.

Where the credential differs by market

Carriers and intermediaries hold different credentials by market, and the one that matters depends on which market you are buying in. A courier's licence says nothing about an ocean booking, and an FMC licence says nothing about parcels.

For the ocean leg, the intermediary arranging carriage into the United States is licensed by the Federal Maritime Commission: the registry held 9,795 organisations as of the 28/07/2026 official export, of which 5,205 carried an active licence number; the registry's own web search page shows a different, larger count because it tallies trade-name listings rather than organisations. The check takes a minute and is set out on which credential covers the ocean leg.

Couriers aside, only 5 of the 20 pages measured cite an industry source of any kind, and none checks a company against a registry. For couriers there is no equivalent registry to check, because the carrier is the brand itself and the terms are its published tariff. For household moves, road authority covers the truck and the ocean leg runs under a bill of lading — two documents, two regimes, as moving your household goods overseas explains.

Running that check on one company takes about two minutes and is free on every market above: how to tell whether a freight forwarder is legit sets out the order the registries are searched in.

Couriers are the market where nothing can be looked up and nothing needs to be: the tariff is published, the carrier is the counterparty, and the claim route is contractual. Every other row has a document that can be read before money moves.

Why no combined ranking exists here

International shipping companies cannot be ranked across the three markets at once, and the reason is not missing data: 0 of 20 pages here cover all three, and 2 cover even two of them. Courier performance is measured in delivery days against a service promise, ocean performance in schedule reliability against a published sailing, and relocation performance in claims and survey accuracy. No metric spans them.

Carriers from different markets therefore get no combined score on this page. Where a market has a measurable basis, the ranking for it lives on its own page, and where it does not, the page says so — that is the same standard applied on every ranking in this project.

Parcels, containers and households route to different pages, and routing is what this page does instead: name the three markets, give the unit each is priced in, and send a reader to the page where the comparison is possible. Duty and destination costs, which apply to every market that is not a courier parcel, are set out under duty and customs fees on top of the freight.

Each market measures performance against a different promise, which is why no column can be shared:

What to ask each type of provider

Couriers, carriers and movers answer different questions, and the shortest path to a comparable quote is asking the right three.

What to ask each type of provider
MarketAsk firstAsk secondAsk third
CourierIs the rate on actual or volumetric weight?What is the declared-value limit?Which service level is quoted?
Ocean containerWhich carrier and which sailing?Which destination charges are excluded?Who issues the bill of lading?
Groupage, LCLWhat is the chargeable volume rule?What does the destination terminal charge?When is the origin cut-off?
RelocationWas the estimate based on a survey?Binding or non-binding?What liability applies at sea?

Carriers answering those questions turn a headline number into a scope. A courier rate quoted on actual weight becomes a different number under volumetric rules; an ocean quote without destination charges is half an invoice; a relocation estimate without a survey is a projection.

Answers of that kind reveal a shipping company's competence faster than a review score does — and 19 of the 20 pages SeaRates measured publish no criteria at all, so the questions are the only comparison tool a reader is given. A provider that cannot name the sailing, the chargeable volume rule or the liability regime is not necessarily dishonest, but it is selling something it does not control. For the container market those questions are already scored: what the forwarder league tables measure reads 62 ranking pages, 3 of which publish a method.

Couriers, carriers and movers each fail differently, so there is no single place to complain either. A courier claim runs under the carrier's published tariff, because the carrier is the counterparty. An ocean claim goes to whoever issued the bill of lading, and if that party is a licensed intermediary its licence and bond are public. A relocation claim runs against a written estimate and the liability option that was signed. Establishing which of the three you are in is the first step in every case, and it is the step these rankings skip.

Who are the best international shipping companies?

The question has three answers because the phrase covers three industries. For parcels: DHL, FedEx, UPS and USPS. For containers: the ocean lines ranked by capacity and reliability. For households: relocation companies with an audited accreditation. Comparing across the three is meaningless.

Is DHL cheaper than sea freight?

For a document or a small parcel, yes, and comparison stops being valid above roughly 30 kg. Couriers price by weight, ocean freight by slot or cubic metre, so the same shipment produces two numbers in two units.

How do I compare carriers properly?

Pick the market first, then compare inside it on one scope: same origin and destination, same unit, same charge list, same delivery terms. Two quotes in different units are not comparable at any price.

Which providers are the most reliable?

Reliability is measured differently per market: on-time arrival for ocean carriers, transit-day performance for couriers, claims and survey accuracy for movers. Ask for the metric before accepting a claim of reliability.

Do I need a licensed company for international shipping?

For the ocean leg arranged by an intermediary, yes — it should appear in the FMC registry. For a courier, the carrier is the counterparty and its tariff is the contract. For a household move, both the road and the ocean legs have their own rules.

Why does one search result mix couriers with container lines?

Because the phrase is ambiguous and Google has not split it. Of the 20 pages ranking here, none covers all three markets, and 6 mix pricing units inside a single comparison.
