MethodologyWhat counts as a source, and when we refuse to publish a number

How every figure on this site is produced, and when we refuse to produce one

By SeaRates · Updated 29 July 2026 · Every figure carries its unit, its reading date and a linked source · Send a correction

⚑ Methodology

Every number here belongs to one of four classes, and the class decides whether it can be published at all. A figure read from a primary record can be published with its reading date. A figure from a secondary source is published with the publisher named. Our own count is published with the corpus and the threshold stated. And a figure we do not hold is named as missing rather than estimated.

The four classes of figure

How figures are classified before publication
ClassWhat it meansHow it is published
PrimaryRead from the record itself — a register, a tariff, a fee scheduleWith the reading date beside it
SecondaryPublished by a named third party — an index, an industry tableWith the publisher and the period named
Our own countMeasured by SeaRates across a stated corpusWith the corpus size, the threshold and the sweep date
Not heldNeeded for a claim, and we do not have itNamed as missing — never estimated to fill the gap

A figure that cannot be placed in one of the first three does not go out. That is why several pages here publish the rows a ranking would need and mark which ones are absent, instead of ranking on adjectives.

How the corpus was read

Before writing, SeaRates captured the full text of pages ranking in US search across 22 question clusters and counted what they contain. The method is stated so the count can be repeated: full page text captured alongside the search results, pages under 200 words excluded, and the threshold for "publishes a method" set at 120 words describing how a result was produced.

  • 2,001 pages read in full across 22 clusters, 07/25-26/2026.
  • 245 pages measured across the ranking clusters; 2 show a licence number.
  • 4,967 unique People Also Ask questions collected; 2,410 survived filtering.

Counts of this kind are our own and are labelled as such. They measure what the market publishes, not whether it is true.

When a figure is refused

Three refusals recur, and each is visible on the site:

  • No average where none exists. Import duty depends on the HTSUS line, the country of origin and the measures in force. The landed-cost calculator takes a duty rate as an input rather than inventing one.
  • No median without an array. SeaRates holds no quote array, so no page here publishes a median rate. Published vendor bands are shown as bands and labelled as bands.
  • No sum of two registers. The FMC forwarder and NVOCC lists overlap — 2,588 organizations hold both roles — so the two counters are published separately and never added.

Dates, and why every figure carries one

The sources behind this site move. The FMC register changed by nine records in the two days between 07/26/2026 and 07/28/2026. Ocean spot rates reprice weekly. A figure from either without its reading date is not a fact about the source, and pages here are built so the date travels with the number rather than sitting in a footer.

Where two sources disagree, both are published with attribution rather than one being chosen. Schedule reliability for mid-2026 is the standing example: Sea-Intelligence and a Kuehne+Nagel summary give different figures for different months, and both appear.

Reputation Score

Company pages carried no score until 12.09.2026. The change followed a Search Console review: real search demand for these pages runs to "[company] ranking", "[company] reddit" and "[company] revenue" — a register row answers none of those, and a correction was owed rather than a rebrand of the same page. The score below is what replaced the page's former "no score" line, not an addition next to it.

Three components, none invented on the day of writing:

Reputation score components and weights
ComponentWeightSource
Independent ratings45%Trustpilot rating, only counted at 30 or more reviews — below that a platform average is an anecdote, not a signal, and SeaRates does not weight anecdotes into a public score
Complaint handling35%Better Business Bureau letter rating, which already weighs complaint volume and resolution — SeaRates converts the letter to points rather than re-weighing what BBB has already weighed
Disclosure20%Whether the company's own site states its FMC/OTI status, and whether it publishes a public rate or accessorial-fee schedule — half the weight each

Authority — the licence, bond and renewal status already on each card — is deliberately excluded from the score, not merely under-weighted. Holding a licence in good standing is the floor for appearing in this directory at all, so it is close to constant across every entity published here; folding a near-constant into a weighted score dilutes the components that actually differ between companies. A licence is still a floor worth stating — it is printed on every card — it is just not treated as a point of comparison.

A score is published only when at least two of the three components carry usable data. Rescaling a single component to fill a 0-100 range manufactures a precision the data does not support: one data point stretched across the full range reads as confident when it is the least confident case there is. Where fewer than two components clear that bar, the card states plainly that no composite is published and prints whatever was found, unscaled, instead.

Two further rules carried over from how every other figure on this site is read. First, a platform rating is captioned to the entity it actually covers: several company brands here operate globally on one Trustpilot domain, and where a rating reflects the whole group rather than the single US-licensed entity on the card, the caption says so rather than letting a global number pass as a US one. Second, where BBB or Trustpilot lists more than one profile for overlapping addresses or affiliated entities, the ratings are not averaged into one figure — each is named, and the card states when the match to this specific licensee is uncertain.

What the score still is not: a recommendation to book, a claim that any individual review is tied to a verified shipment, or a substitute for reading the underlying platform yourself before a deposit leaves your account.
