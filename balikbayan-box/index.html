Which balikbayan box companies actually run this corridor?

LBC Express, Forex Cargo and Atlas Shippers International are the three names that dominate this corridor's search results, and a list of them is not a ranking. Across the 14 result pages SeaRates captured for this cluster, LBC Express appears in 10 (17 mentions), Forex Cargo in 7 (8 mentions), and Atlas Shippers International, UMAC Cargo and Manila Forwarder in 3 each. Names repeat because the corridor is small and the operators are long-established — not because anyone has measured which one delivers for less or arrives sooner.

Ordering these companies would need a price series or a delivery record, and SeaRates has neither: no quote array for balikbayan boxes exists yet, and no transit median has been measured for this corridor. So this page ranks nothing, and puts its numbers where they can be dated instead: the customs side of the shipment.

Brand confusion is a real cost of a market with few names and many imitators. Atlas Shippers International's own site carries a standing notice, captured 09/13/2026: “Atlas Shippers is not affiliated with any other balikbayan cargo company.” A market where the leading operator has to publish that sentence is a market where the storefront name and the company that actually ships the box are worth checking separately, which is exactly what section 5 below covers. Households sending more than a single box, rather than a balikbayan box on its own, run the same fraud check at a larger scale: how international movers compare on our scorecard applies it to full household shipments.

What every cited source agrees on is the practical test that replaces a weight limit: the box closes securely and does not bulge. A box that fails that test is repacked or re-rated at the counter, not measured against a published kilogram ceiling. Households moving more than a box's worth of belongings are priced by volume instead of by box size, and moving your household goods overseas works in cubic metres rather than in box counts.

The customs exemption that decides whether you pay anything at all

A balikbayan box clears free of duty and tax only when the sender qualifies, the contents qualify, and the sender has not already used the privilege up — three separate conditions, not one. SeaRates read this directly at the Bureau of Customs on 09/13/2026, in the agency's own published guidelines.

Who qualifies. Anyone can send a balikbayan box, but only a Qualified Filipino While Abroad — an Overseas Filipino Worker or a resident Filipino citizen temporarily staying abroad — can have it exempted from duty and tax. A non-Filipino sender, or a Filipino who does not fall into either category, has no exemption to claim.

What qualifies. The box must contain only personal and household effects that do not exceed personal and normal needs. Goods for resale or barter do not qualify, whoever sends them.

The cap. The exemption applies only up to three shipments a calendar year, and only while the declared value stays at or under ₱150,000 per shipment. A fourth shipment in the same year, or a box valued above the cap, loses the exemption and duties and taxes are collected on it.

The paperwork. The sender submits an Information Sheet (available from the Bureau of Customs' own site or from the consolidator), a photocopy of the passport's biographical page, and an invoice or receipt if one exists, to the consolidator abroad. The consolidator passes these documents to the Bureau of Customs and to the receiving freight forwarder in the Philippines. The box is examined, duties and taxes are assessed if the conditions above are not met, and only then is it released for delivery.

What is separately restricted. Prohibited items cannot be imported regardless of the exemption. Regulated or restricted items need a permit from the relevant regulatory agency before they can be imported at all — the exemption covers duty and tax, not the underlying restriction. Bureau of Customs guidance is explicit that importing prohibited goods, or regulated goods without the required permit, can lead to confiscation or imprisonment under the Customs Modernization and Tariff Act.

Legal basis for all of the above: Republic Act 10863, the Customs Modernization and Tariff Act (CMTA), Section 800(g); Customs Administrative Order (CAO) 1-2018; Customs Memorandum Order (CMO) 18-2018.

Two registries, not one: who's allowed to move the box

A balikbayan box crosses two regulatory regimes — the US ocean leg and Philippine deconsolidation — and none of the vendor pages in this cluster names either one.

The ocean leg needs an FMC Ocean Transportation Intermediary licence. The Federal Maritime Commission's registry held 9,795 organisations as of the 28/07/2026 official export, of which 5,205 carried an active licence number. Ask which company actually issues the bill of lading for the sea leg, then look that name up rather than the storefront's — the same check the licence and bond behind the company name runs for every other money page on this site.

The Philippine side needs accreditation to deconsolidate. The Bureau of Customs publishes its own list of accredited cargo forwarders for consolidated balikbayan box shipments, organised per port of entry (Manila International Container Port, Port of Manila, NAIA, and 13 other ports), and states plainly that stakeholders should “only transact with accredited cargo forwarders… to avoid fraud.” This is a second, separate check from the FMC one — a company can hold a valid US ocean licence and still not be the accredited party clearing the box on the Philippine side.

What goes wrong: the cap, the paperwork, and the wrong name on the receipt

Balikbayan box shipments fail for a short, predictable list of reasons, and every one of them is checkable before the box ships.

  • The cap is already used up. A sender's fourth box in a calendar year, or a box valued over ₱150,000, loses the duty-free exemption outright — there is no partial exemption on the excess.
  • The paperwork is incomplete. Without the Information Sheet, the passport photocopy, or the receipt where one exists, the Bureau of Customs has nothing to match the sender's claim of eligibility against.
  • The forwarder is not accredited. Fraud around unaccredited or impersonating operators is common enough that both the Bureau of Customs and individual companies such as Atlas Shippers publish standing warnings about it.
  • The declared contents or value do not match reality. Bureau of Customs guidance asks senders to declare true and correct items and value; a mismatch turns an exemption claim into a customs investigation.
  • A regulated item has no permit. The exemption removes duty and tax — it does not remove the requirement that a regulated item be cleared with its own agency's permit first.

Every one of these is a document or a name to check, not a risk to absorb after the box has sailed.

How many balikbayan boxes can I send duty-free per year?

Up to three shipments per calendar year, and only if each stays at or under ₱150,000 in declared value — both conditions apply together, per the Bureau of Customs' own guidelines. A fourth shipment, or any shipment over the value cap, is dutiable.

Who qualifies for the balikbayan box duty exemption?

What documents do I need to send a balikbayan box duty-free?

What size is a balikbayan box?

How do I check if a balikbayan box forwarder is legitimate?

Two separate checks. For the US ocean leg, ask which company issues the bill of lading and look up its FMC Ocean Transportation Intermediary licence. For Philippine clearance, check the Bureau of Customs' own published list of accredited cargo forwarders for consolidated balikbayan box shipments — the agency states this directly as its own advice against fraud.
