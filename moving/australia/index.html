What does it cost to ship household goods to Australia from USA?

A 20ft container to Australia is published at $3,000 to $12,000 before destination charges, and a 40ft at $9,000 to $18,000. SeaRates read both ranges on 07/26/2026. A fourfold spread inside one box size is not a price for household goods — containers of the same length carry very different moves, and the spread is the sum of four variables nobody separates.

Volume, not budget, is the decision.

20ft, 40ft or a shared container: where the saving flips

Shared-container loads start at $3,000 while a sole-use 40ft reaches $18,000, and the decision between them is a volume decision rather than a budget one. Groupage means buying space by volume inside someone else's container, and the price of household goods follows cubic metres rather than the steel around them. Shared containers move several households at once.

The switch point between groupage and a sole-use container cannot be calculated from this corpus. Cubic-metre pricing appears on exactly 1 of 51 pages measured on 07/26/2026, which means the market publishes totals and hides the unit. SeaRates has therefore not derived the threshold and does not present one.

Household goods volume is measured twice — once by the surveyor who quotes the move and once by the crew who loads the container. A quote built on an optimistic survey is the commonest way the final invoice exceeds the estimate.

Household goods loaded into a sole-use container also travel without a second handling: the box is sealed at the origin address and opened at destination, whereas groupage is unpacked and repacked at a consolidation warehouse on each end. Owners of fragile or high-value belongings frequently pay the sole-use premium for that reason alone, and the trade-off never appears in a price comparison because it is not a price.

The departure coast moves the bill more than the box size

West Coast sailings run $6,500 to $7,500 against $14,000 to $16,000 from the East, for the same 20ft container on the New Zealand corridor. A gap of more than two to one sits inside a variable most household goods pages compress into the phrase "depends on your location". Containers leaving from different coasts are the same steel at very different prices.

What door-to-door covers, and where it actually ends

Door-to-door on household goods stops at the destination door and never covers duty or inspection, which is the single most common misreading of an international moving quote. Port-to-port ends earlier still, at the destination terminal, leaving clearance and inland delivery to the owner.

Duty relief: how long you must have lived abroad, and on which form

Household goods relief is tied to residence: Canada grants settler status after one year abroad, and the UK runs it through ToR1. Personal effects and household goods usually enter duty-free when the owner is genuinely relocating, but every regime attaches that relief to a named form and a stated period, and missing either turns a free entry into a taxed one.

Household goods entering Canada follow a defined status: Canada Border Services defines a settler as somebody entering to establish residence for one year or more, and a former resident as somebody returning after a continuous absence of a year or more; both routes run through Memorandum D2-2-1, Settlers' Effects. US Customs and Border Protection requires Form 3299 for unaccompanied household effects and Form 6059B for what travels with you, and states that people living in the United States for only part of the year must file 3299 as well.

Household goods relief regimes are the least-covered part of this market: 11 of 51 pages measured on 07/26/2026 mention any relief regime at all. Owners who want the arithmetic of what duty would cost without relief can work through duty and customs fees on top of the freight.

Transfer of Residence in the United Kingdom is an application, not an automatic status. Household goods arriving before the approval comes back are treated as an ordinary import, and the relief cannot be applied retrospectively to a consignment already cleared. Owners who ship first and apply later discover the sequence at the worst moment.

Timing is the part that costs money.

What cannot go in the container

Personal effects entering Australia meet a published list: fresh produce, live plants, bulbs and unidentified seeds are named as prohibited, and the list comes from the Department of Agriculture rather than from a mover's summary of it. Prohibited items are not a formality — one non-compliant item is enough to hold the whole container.

Personal effects entering Australia are prohibited or restricted when they include:

  • fresh fruit and vegetables;
  • live plants, bulbs, tubers, corms and cuttings;
  • prohibited and restricted seeds, and unidentified seeds — including spices;
  • plant products flagged as high risk for khapra beetle;
  • live animals.

Food inside household goods splits three ways: some items are permitted, others prohibited outright, and a third group allowed only after treatment. Household goods lists published by movers reproduce the headline categories and drop the awkward detail: 19 of 51 pages measured on 07/26/2026 list prohibited items, and the spice clause — the one that catches ordinary kitchens — appears in almost none of them.

Personal effects lists published by movers also flatten a distinction the department keeps: prohibited items cannot arrive at all, restricted items can arrive under conditions, and a third group is admissible only after treatment at the owner's expense. A wicker basket is not in the same category as a bag of soil, and a household packing for Australia needs to know which of its own possessions sit in which tier.

How the Australian biosecurity bill is actually calculated

Biosecurity inspection charges on household goods are based on time, so packing decides part of the invoice. The Department of Agriculture assesses the documents accompanying a personal effects consignment and frequently requires the goods to be presented for inspection; the documentation is what decides how much of the consignment has to be opened.

Household goods that arrive well documented and cleanly packed take less inspector time, and inspector time is the billing unit. Australian biosecurity charging therefore works in the opposite direction to freight: freight rewards volume efficiency, biosecurity rewards clarity.

Treatment of personal effects is the second cost, and it is not always avoidable. The department warns that some items require treatment before they arrive in Australia, and that owners who insist on keeping a particular item may have to pay treatment fees for it. A wooden garden bench and a barbecue with soil on the feet are not exotic edge cases — they are the ordinary contents of a garage.

Quarantine appears on 17 of 51 pages measured by SeaRates on 07/26/2026, and not one of them explains how the charge is calculated. Naming the mechanism is the difference between a warning and an instruction.

Biosecurity assessment also runs on the paperwork before anything is opened. The department states that it assesses the documents accompanying a personal effects import and then decides how much of the consignment must be presented, which puts the decision about inspection scope upstream of the inspection itself. Household goods that arrive with a precise, itemised declaration give the assessor a reason to sample rather than to open everything.

That single sentence is the whole cost mechanism.

The inventory decides whether the container gets opened

A vague inventory line turns a document check into a full container inspection, which is why the inventory is a customs document rather than a packing aid. An assessment that cannot tell what is in box 47 has one reliable way to find out.

Writing an inventory that survives assessment takes four habits:

  1. Name the item, not the category. "Kitchen items" invites a look; "ceramic plates, 12" does not.
  2. Flag anything organic before it is found. Wooden furniture, wicker, plant pots and leather all have a treatment path if declared and a problem if discovered.
  3. Give values that match the item. A declared value wildly below replacement cost reads as an undeclared commercial shipment.
  4. Keep the numbering consistent between the packing list, the inventory and the declaration.

Inventories appear on 23 of 51 pages measured on 07/26/2026 — nearly half the market mentions them — and none connects inventory quality to inspection cost. Personal effects paperwork is treated as an administrative chore rather than as the lever it is.

Who is licensed for the ocean leg of your move?

Household goods pages skip the ocean licence entirely: not one of 51 ranking for this cluster names it. SeaRates swept the full text of every competing page on 07/26/2026: zero mention the Federal Maritime Commission or an OTI licence, and 5 (9.8%) name a USDOT number, which governs a truck between US states and says nothing about the water.

An international move is sold as one service and performed by several companies. An FMC Ocean Transportation Intermediary licence covers arranging the ocean carriage; FIDI and FAIM cover audited quality in the moving trade; a USDOT number covers the domestic truck. Moving companies hold different combinations of the three, and the credential has to match the leg that worries you.

Household goods entering Canada sit under a memorandum rather than a web page, and the distinction matters when a broker and a mover disagree. Memorandum D2-2-1, Settlers' Effects, is the operational document Canada Border Services officers work from, and it separates the settler — somebody arriving to establish residence for a year or more — from the former resident returning after a continuous absence of a year or more. Each status carries its own evidence requirements and its own treatment of goods acquired abroad, which is why an answer that begins "you can bring your household goods duty-free" is true only until somebody asks which of the two you are, how long you were away, and when each item was acquired. Personal effects that were bought during the final months abroad are the ones that generate questions, and the inventory is where that timeline either exists or does not.

What goes wrong: re-measured volume, opened containers, storage

Household goods volume re-measured at loading is the commonest way the quote changes, and the crew that measures is not the person who quoted. International moves fail in a short list of predictable ways, each with a check that costs nothing beforehand.

  • Container volume grows on the day. Ask whether the survey was physical or video, and whether the quote is fixed or subject to re-measurement.
  • The container is opened at inspection. Documentation quality drives inspector time, and inspector time drives the charge.
  • An item fails biosecurity. Treatment, re-export or destruction — all three are billed to the owner.
  • Clearance stalls and storage starts. Storage and quay rent accrue while paperwork is fixed; the mechanics are the same as the difference between demurrage and detention on commercial cargo.
  • Insurance turns out to be a valuation limit. Insurance is mentioned on 11 of 51 pages measured on 07/26/2026, and not one states the basis on which a claim is calculated.

Personal effects claims are settled against the declared inventory, which closes the loop: the document that keeps the container shut is the same document that decides what a loss is worth.

Insurance on household goods deserves the same scepticism as the freight quote. Cover sold alongside an international move is usually written against the declared inventory value, with exclusions for items packed by the owner rather than by the crew, and with a deductible that makes small claims pointless. Owners who pack their own boxes to save money frequently discover that the saving moved the risk onto themselves.

Sea or air: the point where the price difference stops mattering

Air freight wins on urgency and loses on volume, and the switch between them is a volume decision rather than a preference. Air pricing follows chargeable weight, so a light bulky household is punished, while a small dense shipment of documents, instruments or tools travels cheaply.

Air freight comparisons appear on 7 of 51 pages measured on 07/26/2026, mostly as a sentence rather than a threshold. A useful rule of thumb is direction rather than a number: below roughly the volume of a few suitcases, air is competitive; above a full room, sea wins decisively and the only question left is which box.

Container transit medians and p90 for this corridor have not been measured by SeaRates, so the shipping times published elsewhere are not reproduced here as findings. International moving timelines are best planned from the sailing schedule backwards, with clearance treated as variable rather than fixed.

Air freight for a household move is priced on chargeable weight, which is the greater of actual weight and volumetric weight, and that single rule explains most of the confusion around it. A box of books is dense and cheap to fly relative to its size; a box of duvets and lampshades is light and expensive, because the airline charges for the space it occupies rather than the mass it adds. Sea freight inverts the logic entirely, pricing a sealed container by the box regardless of what is inside it, and pricing groupage by the cubic metre with no weight penalty at ordinary household densities. Households comparing the two are therefore not comparing one price against another but one pricing model against a different one, and the answer flips depending on what is actually being moved rather than on how much of it there is.

Published ranges read $3,000 to $12,000 for a 20ft container from the USA on 07/26/2026, and $9,000 to $18,000 for a 40ft. Both figures exclude duty, biosecurity inspection and treatment. SeaRates publishes these as ranges, not as a median of quotes paid.

What can I not ship in a container overseas?

For Australia, the Department of Agriculture prohibits or restricts fresh fruit and vegetables, live plants, bulbs, tubers, corms and cuttings, prohibited and unidentified seeds including spices, plant products high risk for khapra beetle, and live animals. Other destinations publish their own lists.

How is the Australian biosecurity charge calculated?

Personal effects are charged by inspection time. The department assesses the accompanying documents, decides how much of the consignment to inspect, and bills the inspection on a time basis. Better documentation and cleaner packing reduce inspector time and therefore the charge.

What is the difference between door-to-door and port-to-port?

Port-to-port ends at the destination terminal and leaves clearance and delivery to you. Door-to-door adds collection and final delivery. Neither includes duty, biosecurity inspection or treatment, and neither covers storage if clearance stalls.

Which form do I file for household goods entering the United States?

CBP Form 3299 for unaccompanied household articles, and Form 6059B for goods travelling with you. People who live in the United States for only part of the year also have to file Form 3299 to import unaccompanied household effects.
