What makes an FBA shipment different from an import

Amazon FBA freight forwarder rankings rarely explain the part that actually breaks: a shipment to a fulfilment centre is not a normal import with a different address. All nine pages cover the mechanics, which is the highest topical coverage SeaRates has measured in any ranking cluster.

FBA freight is accepted or refused on three constraints, and 8 of 9 pages in the corpus cover at least one of them:

  • The inbound plan. Goods must arrive against a plan created in Seller Central, carrying its shipment ID and FNSKU labelling. Cartons without correct labels are refused or charged for relabelling.
  • The delivery appointment. Fulfilment centres accept freight in booked windows. A missed window sends the truck away, and the container sits accruing storage while a new slot is found.
  • Carton and pallet rules. Weight limits, labelling placement and pallet configuration are specified by Amazon, and non-compliant freight is rejected at the door rather than at the border.

Freight forwarders without FBA experience clear customs correctly and then discover the appointment system. A seller comparing quotes should ask how many fulfilment centre deliveries the company booked last month — the answer is either a number or a change of subject. Sellers shipping less than a full container land in a different market again: how consolidators price cargo by the cubic metre reads the 23 pages ranking for it, and none shows a method.

Sellers consolidating into a shared box hit the same weight-or-measure rule as any other LCL shipper, and where the LCL saving disappears puts the crossover at 13-15 CBM.

Who is the importer, and why Amazon will not be

Freight forwarders raise importer of record on 2 of the 9 pages measured, which puts it level with the FMC licence and with the customs bond and leaves seven pages of the nine silent on who is named on the entry. Amazon does not act as importer of record for seller inventory, and freight consigned to a fulfilment centre as importer will not be accepted.

A foreign seller therefore needs one of two things: a US legal entity able to act as importer of record, or a service arrangement where a broker or forwarder takes that role for the entry. Only 2 of the 9 pages measured say so. The choice decides who holds the customs bond, who receives CBP correspondence, and who is liable if a declaration is later found wrong.

Freight forwarders selling DDP return the same question in a sharper form. A DDP quote means somebody else files the entry and pays the duty, and if the seller never sees the entry number, the declared value and the HTS code are outside its control while the liability may not be. Most FBA cargo starts in China, and who files the entry on a DDP shipment puts the same question to the 31 pages ranking on that lane.

The filings that happen before anything reaches a warehouse

Freight forwarders file Importer Security Filing for US-bound ocean cargo, a requirement in force since 26 January 2009, and it is filed before the container is loaded at origin rather than on arrival. Late or missing filings bring monetary penalties, increased examination and delay — all of which land on the inbound plan.

The customs bond an FBA importer needs is sized on last year's activity: 10% of duties, taxes and fees paid over the previous 12 months, rounded up to the nearest $10,000, with a $50,000 minimum. A seller whose volume or duty rate rose during the year can find the bond insufficient and the goods held while it is increased.

Amazon FBA freight forwarder rankings barely mention either filing: 1 page of the 9 names Importer Security Filing and 2 pages name the customs bond, with a single page covering both. An earlier count of zero was corrected on 07/28/2026 by re-reading the corpus page by page — forwarderone.com states the ISF deadline and offers bond management, and junglescout.com explains what the bond secures. Both are answerable by asking the forwarder which entity files what, and both are the seller's problem when the answer turns out to be nobody. Which gateway the container clears through changes the cost behind both filings: what this port costs and how long boxes sit there prices it across 183 measured pages.

Which licence covers the ocean leg

Two of nine pages say a forwarder is FMC-licensed, none prints the licence number, and one tells the reader to check registration numbers rather than publishing a check of its own. Freight forwarders arranging ocean carriage into the United States are licensed by the Federal Maritime Commission; the registry held 9,795 organisations as of the 28/07/2026 official export, of which 5,205 carried an active licence number; the registry's own web search page shows a different, larger count because it tallies trade-name listings rather than organisations.

Is Amazon a freight forwarder?

Can I use DDP for FBA shipments?

It is common and it moves the entry out of your sight. Ask for the entry number and the name of the importer of record; if the seller cannot see the declared value and HTS code, it cannot verify the duty paid on its own goods.

What happens if my shipment misses its delivery appointment?

The fulfilment centre turns the truck away and the freight waits for a new window, accruing storage and, for containerised cargo, potentially demurrage. Rebooking depends on the forwarder's relationship with the carrier and the centre.

Does an FBA forwarder need an FMC licence?

If it arranges ocean carriage into the US or issues a bill of lading there, yes. A prep centre or a labelling service does not need one, and the two roles are often sold together under one brand.

Why does one company's page rank for every FBA forwarder query?

Because the field is nine pages and that company publishes the most complete one. It is legitimate content marketing; it also means the search result contains no independent view, which is why this page publishes counts instead of a ranking.
