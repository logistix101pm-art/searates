DDP is the product being sold, and the one nobody explains

Thirteen of 31 pages sell DDP — delivered duty paid — and the same corpus explains what it means for the buyer on almost none of them. Under DDP, the seller or its forwarder handles export, ocean carriage, the US entry and delivery, and quotes one all-in number per kilo or per container.

The question that decides an importer's exposure is who is named as importer of record on the entry. If it is the forwarder or a party arranged by the seller, the US buyer never sees the declared value, the HTS code or the duty actually paid, and the compliance record sits with somebody else.

Pages in this cluster describe how cheap DDP is produced: declared values marked below the real ones, HTS codes chosen for a lower rate, goods claimed under the de minimis threshold when they do not qualify, and shipments sent without a full customs file. One source in the corpus states plainly that those practices amount to smuggling whatever the supplier believes.

Freight forwarders quoting an all-in DDP rate below the duty alone are not offering efficiency; they are offering a declaration you cannot see. Asking for the entry number after clearance, and checking it, is the whole defence. Sellers shipping into Amazon warehouses meet the same DDP question with an extra party attached: which forwarders handle FBA prep and appointments reads the 9 pages ranking for it.

A DDP price is also not a statement about the service mode. Door-to-door describes where the truck stops; DDP describes who pays the duty, and a quote can be one without being the other. Asking for the entry number after clearance settles both questions at once, because the entry names the importer of record and carries the value that was declared.

Tariffs move faster than the bond behind them

Freight forwarders on the lane mention tariffs, Section 301 or the de minimis threshold on 14 of the 31 pages, which makes it the most-covered compliance topic here and still a minority of the corpus. The number that changes quietly is not the duty rate but the customs bond.

A continuous bond is sized at 10% of the duties, taxes and fees paid over the previous 12 months, rounded up to the nearest $10,000 with a $50,000 minimum. Sufficiency is therefore calculated on last year's payments — so a tariff increase this year leaves the bond short, CBP issues an insufficiency notice, and cargo stops until the bond is raised.

China to USA freight forwarder rankings do not check this, and no importer should assume the forwarder is watching it either. The bond belongs to the importer of record, which loops back to the previous section: if you are not the importer of record, you do not control the instrument your goods are released against. Once the box lands, the charge that accrues fastest is the terminal clock: how the demurrage and detention clock runs sets out the 3 to 7 free days and what day eight costs.

A freight forwarder's rate is the visible half of that bill and rarely the larger one: why shipping from China costs what it does separates the ocean line from the surcharges, the federal fees and the duty.

The filing that has to happen before the ship sails

China to USA freight forwarder rankings skip the filing that decides whether the container loads at all: Importer Security Filing, ISF-10, has applied to US-bound ocean cargo since 26 January 2009, and a late or missing filing brings penalties, examination and delay. US compliance was counted on this corpus by two topics, not by all of them: tariffs with Section 301 and the de minimis threshold, on 14 of the 31 pages, and the FMC licence behind the ocean leg, on 2 — neither of which prints the number. ISF itself was not counted, so the corpus is silent on how many of the 31 name it. It is filed before the container is loaded at origin, not on arrival.

Freight forwarders on this lane split the work in a way that decides who is accountable: the Chinese office books the vessel, the US office or a customs broker files ISF and the entry. A forwarder that cannot say which entity performs the US filings has not organised the shipment; it has sold the ocean leg and left the rest.

Who is licensed for the US side

Chinese forwarders are not licensed by the United States, and they do not have to be — but the party arranging ocean carriage into the US does, if it is issuing a bill of lading or acting as an intermediary there.

Who is the best freight forwarder from China to the USA?

No page in this search result answers that with anything checkable, and neither does this one. The names that recur most are Flexport, AEB Logistics, Sinotrans, Unicargo and Freightos — a frequency count, not a ranking.

Is DDP shipping from China safe?

It is legal and common; the risk is invisibility. If you are not the importer of record you do not see the declared value, the HTS code or the duty paid, and an undervalued entry is your exposure once CBP reviews it. Ask for the entry number and check it.

What is ISF and who files it?

Importer Security Filing, required for US-bound ocean cargo since 26 January 2009 and filed before loading at origin. Usually the customs broker or the US arm of your forwarder files it. Late or missing filings bring penalties and examinations.

Why did my shipment stop over a bond?

Because a continuous bond is sized on last year's duties — 10% of them, rounded up, minimum $50,000 — and a tariff increase leaves it short. CBP issues an insufficiency notice and cargo waits until the bond is raised.

Does a Chinese forwarder need a US licence?

Not for work performed in China. For arranging carriage into the US as an intermediary, or for issuing a bill of lading there, the FMC licence applies — which is why the US partner's name matters more than the origin office's brochure.

How do I compare two China to USA quotes?

Put them in the same unit and the same scope: same Incoterm, same container size, same ports, destination charges listed line by line, and the same answer to who files ISF and the entry. Rates alone are not comparable across different scopes.
